# The Professor's Lab: the story of UNIGINE Superposition
# Speaker lines start with ALEX: or MORGAN:

## Cold open
ALEX: A lone professor, an abandoned classroom, and a loud bang from the laboratory a few moments ago.
MORGAN: That is the actual premise of a graphics card benchmark. Your job is to look around and work out what happened.
ALEX: Today: UNIGINE Superposition, the benchmark that has been pushing graphics cards since the eleventh of April, two thousand and seventeen.

## The family
MORGAN: Start with who made it. UNIGINE was established in two thousand and five, and its real product is a real-time three D engine, used for simulation, games and virtual reality.
ALEX: But the benchmarks are what gamers know. Sanctuary in two thousand and seven. Tropics in two thousand and eight. Heaven in two thousand and nine. Valley in two thousand and thirteen. And Superposition in two thousand and seventeen.
MORGAN: Heaven is the one that made the name. On the twenty-second of October, two thousand and nine, UNIGINE announced it as the world's first true DirectX eleven benchmark.
ALEX: The pitch was tessellation. A cobblestone street that looked flat in DirectX nine suddenly had real depth in DirectX eleven. One reviewer even showed the wireframe, because the wireframe was the whole point.
MORGAN: And it supported AMD's Eyefinity for multiple monitors, which was a big deal for the enthusiasts of that moment.

## A long wait
ALEX: Superposition did not arrive on time. It was announced in late twenty sixteen with a promise to ship by the end of that year.
MORGAN: Instead it slipped, and landed on the eleventh of April, twenty seventeen. Overclock3D and TechPowerUp both ran it that day, and a lot of other sites picked it up on the twelfth.
ALEX: It ran on the second generation of the UNIGINE engine, which is the real technical jump from Valley and Heaven.
MORGAN: Early reviews called it very demanding. One Linux reviewer watched the work spread evenly across every one of his processor cores. He also hit rough edges: the Linux build offered only OpenGL, with no Vulkan, and it saw only three gigabytes of his eight gigabyte card.

## What you actually do with it
MORGAN: Here is how it works. There are presets, each for a class of machine. Ten eighty P Medium for entry-level gaming PCs and laptops. Ten eighty P High for the mid-range. Ten eighty P Extreme and four K Optimized for the high end.
ALEX: And then eight K Optimized. UNIGINE's own manual describes that one as, quote, future configurations, from two thousand and seventeen. An eight K benchmark, in a year when most people were still arguing about four K.
MORGAN: One odd choice: there is no single overall score. Every test and preset gives its own number, and you are told not to compare across presets.
ALEX: There is also a virtual reality readiness test, capped at ten thousand points, which tells you whether your card can run a headset at that quality. That one is kept out of the leaderboards.
MORGAN: And you choose your graphics interface, DirectX or OpenGL.

## The lab itself
ALEX: The scene is a cluttered lab belonging to that fictional professor, and the headline technology is called SSRTGI. Screen-space ray-traced global illumination.
MORGAN: Careful with that name. It works in screen space. It is UNIGINE's own dynamic lighting trick, not the hardware ray tracing that Nvidia's RTX cards brought in twenty eighteen.
ALEX: Which is why Superposition was never a ray tracing benchmark. The first dedicated one for gamers was 3DMark Port Royal, in January twenty nineteen.
MORGAN: Beyond the scripted run there is an interactive mode with mini-games and over nine hundred objects you can pick up and throw. Darts are in there. It turns out there was once a crash caused by throwing darts, and it got a line in the release notes.
ALEX: There is also a loop stress test, built to find out whether your card, your cooling and your power supply stay stable for hours, not minutes.

## Three editions
MORGAN: Superposition comes in three editions. Basic is free, with single runs, settings, GPU monitoring and the virtual reality readiness test.
ALEX: Advanced adds history, the stress test and the online leaderboards, for personal use. Pro adds command-line automation, reports, per-frame statistics, technical support and commercial use, which is how hardware makers use it on a production line.
MORGAN: It runs on Windows seven service pack one and up, and on Linux. Notably not on Mac. UNIGINE support once told a forum user that macOS lacks the OpenGL four point five core profile the engine needed.

## Version one point one
ALEX: In April of twenty nineteen, version one point one arrived, and it is still the latest listed in UNIGINE's own version history.
MORGAN: The headline: free virtual reality in the Basic edition, which used to be paid. Plus virtual reality on Linux through SteamVR.
ALEX: They also raised the maximum render resolution to sixteen thousand three hundred eighty-four pixels square, added a Vive Pro preset, and updated the hardware database so new cards were detected properly.
MORGAN: And a handful of fixes, including a wrong setting at three thousand four hundred forty by fourteen hundred forty, a splash screen bug on ultrawide monitors, and, yes, the dart crash.
ALEX: UNIGINE also shared its first two years of numbers. Over two hundred thousand competitive scores, more than eight thousand benchmark videos on YouTube, and three million, three hundred thirty-eight thousand, nine hundred twenty-three tests run.
MORGAN: Plus a claim that an eight K demo was broadcast from space by satellite. Those are the company's own figures, but they paint the picture of a benchmark that overclockers truly adopted.

## Why it stuck
ALEX: Superposition sits in a useful niche. It is free, it runs on Linux, it has a stress test, and it is pretty enough to leave running while you tune a card.
MORGAN: And it is the last stop of a line that began with a rendered sanctuary in two thousand and seven. Five benchmarks, one engine, and a professor who is still, as far as anyone can tell, mid-experiment.
ALEX: The full research and every source are in the repo. Thanks for listening.
