The vanished professor: scene and lore

The abandoned classroom and its missing physicist.

Research: 13 November 2026. Adds visual and narrative investigation to the thirty scene questions and existing Superposition history. This is UNIGINE's 2017 graphics benchmark, not unrelated games and books also titled Superposition.

Read this first: Unlike FurMark, Superposition has a real developer-written story hook, but it is intentionally brief. Canon = UNIGINE says it; observed = visible promotional scene or documented review; interpretation = plausible reading; unverified = no dependable published answer identified. We did not play the executable or inspect its proprietary assets in this research pass.

1. Who is the professor? Does he have a name, biography or identity?

Canon: UNIGINE describes a lone professor who repeatedly performs dangerous experiments in an abandoned classroom. He is obsessed with spectacular inventions and discoveries and wants to prove his ideas S1. The official manual repeats that premise and, in its Game Mode instructions, also calls him 'the physicist' S2. He is absent when the player arrives, which is the reason to investigate.

Unverified: UNIGINE's product page, game-mode manual, original promotional copy and artist showcase do not name him, identify his university, provide a biography, or confirm his fate S1S2S3. 'Professor Einstein', 'Professor Tesla', 'a mad scientist named X' or 'a real scientist' would be made-up identities. Professor/physicist is the defensible description.

Character reading (interpretation): His personality is revealed through absence: his obsessive experiments and dense accumulation of notebooks and apparatus make his work, not his portrait, the protagonist.

2. Why an abandoned classroom instead of a sleek high-tech laboratory?

Canon: The classroom is explicitly abandoned; the professor has repurposed it as a working laboratory S1. No UNIGINE artist statement found explains the exact artistic decision.

Observed: Official scene pictures show tall windows, old brick and timber surfaces, a classroom/lecture-hall quality, blackboards, papers and clocks alongside imposing coils, cylinders, cables and experimental equipment S3S4. The aesthetic reads as vintage academic science crossed with steampunk-like invention. 'Steampunk' is a visual description, not a separately documented story setting or official genre designation.

Interpretation: An old classroom provides visual contrast between everyday education and dangerous experimentation. Books and clutter create environmental storytelling, while tall windows and many surfaces challenge the engine with natural light, self-shadowing and material detail. We do not know why the fictional building was vacated, who owned it or whether it is in a particular country.

3. What happened immediately before the visitor arrives?

Canon: The visitor reaches the site early in the morning, the professor is missing, and an unexplained loud bang was heard from the laboratory moments before S1S2. Those are the only directly documented sequence-of-events facts in the plot introduction.

Not established: Whether an explosion occurred, what device caused it, whether the professor teleported, was injured, escaped, entered a different universe or simply walked away. 'Bang' does not by itself prove any of those. Official wording frames the event as a mystery to investigate, not an already-solved tragedy.

Interpretation: This is a 'missing person / interrupted experiment' narrative hook. The apparently mundane classroom becomes ominous because its operator has disappeared just when an experiment went wrong—or at least when something made a loud noise.

4. Why does the story invoke quantum theory? Does the title 'Superposition' reveal the plot?

Canon: UNIGINE directly asks visitors to investigate the mystery by looking at the professor's records and instruments, with specific reference to quantum theory S1. The title Superposition is a genuine physics term referring to systems described as combinations of possible states before a particular measurement.

Plausible reading, not confirmed plot: 'Superposition' evokes overlapping states, simultaneous possibilities, and ambiguous reality; these fit a missing scientist and an experimental catastrophe. The title is thematically apt, but no verified UNIGINE explanation identifies an exact quantum experiment or states that the professor was split into parallel copies. Such stories may be fun fan theories, not established canon.

A benchmark rendering a convincing laboratory does not itself simulate scientifically accurate quantum physics. The GPU work concerns lighting, geometry, textures and physics interactions, not a reproducible laboratory demonstration of quantum superposition S2S5.

5. What are the strange machines, rings, coils and pages?

Observed: The producer's own ArtStation showcase displays a complicated large central cylindrical apparatus with protruding red components, heavy cabling, older bench instruments, metal rings, models, papers, books and classroom fittings S3. Contemporary review screenshots show optical/electrical-style equipment, gauges, a cluttered bench, furniture and chalkboards S4.

Canon is limited: The maker calls these records and instruments, relevant to inspecting the professor's work S1. In the sources reviewed, UNIGINE does not publish a part-numbered schematic assigning a fictional function to every individual machine. A visually Tesla-coil-like device is not confirmed to be 'the quantum teleporter' or 'the accident generator'.

Design interpretation: Distinct silhouettes and materials—glass, metal, paper, wood, cabling—make an environment varied enough to demonstrate modern rendering while encouraging players to ask what each device does.

6. Which items are confirmed clues and which are just props?

Canon: UNIGINE specifically singles out the professor's written records and instruments as things worth inspecting S1. That is a meaningful directive from the maker. The manual calls the setting a mystery of the physicist and encourages looking in every corner S2.

But: The public manual does not provide an official itemised clue list, solved puzzle transcript, order of evidence or complete object-to-story glossary. A blackboard equation, portrait, notebook, peculiar electrical device or dartboard may carry visual meaning; without a supporting caption, readable in-game text, a developer statement or a documented puzzle solution, we cannot label it a specific plot clue.

Interpretation: The set dressing helps tell a story in the same way a film set does. The crucial research distinction is between 'looks like a clue' and 'developer confirms this reveals what happened'.

7. Can you actually be a detective and interact with the scene?

Confirmed: Yes, there is a playable Game Mode, not merely a fly-through. UNIGINE's version 1.1 user manual invites players to explore, investigate the physicist's mystery, and interact with more than 900 objects S2. The listed features include physical object interactions, the ability to move and look around, and moving between game and cinematic modes. The manual maps the left mouse button to grabbing/releasing objects and describes keyboard, controller and VR controls S2.

UNIGINE also advertises mini-games. Its 2019 update notably fixed a crash when throwing darts, an unusually tangible sign that the professor's laboratory is deliberately toy-like as well as cinematic S5. Promotional artwork reinforces the emphasis on a believable, cluttered workspace S3.

Limit: Interactive objects, physics and mini-games do not independently prove a solvable hidden main quest or a canonical alternate ending. The manual's invitation is real; the answer to what solves the mystery remains unverified here.

8. How do gravity, time and lighting relate to the story?

Confirmed Game Mode controls: UNIGINE documents a tweakable day/night cycle, gravity control, adjustable time scale, room lighting adjustments and the option to switch to cinematic mode S2. Those are unusually creative features for a benchmark.

Aesthetic interpretation: Moving through the room at different times and light levels can reveal previously hidden details. Turning gravity down produces floating instruments and books; a playful visual analogue to a physics experiment and a useful demonstration of the engine's physical interactions. It is not confirmed that changing gravity unlocks a plot revelation or that time control lets you undo the bang.

The technological purpose is clearer: the same setting can show changing direct/screen-space illumination and many dynamic objects S6. Keep the gameplay/physics controls separate from the title's quantum metaphor.

9. Is there an official ending, proof of where he went, or a solution to the mystery?

The honest result is unresolved. UNIGINE's marketing copy announces the mystery and its manual encourages solving it, but the primary material reviewed does not disclose an explicit final cutscene, named culprit, device activation, answer or official ending S1S2S3. The available changelog describes software features and bug fixes, not story chapters or narrative updates S5.

This does not prove no player has ever worked something out in the executable; we did not complete an interactive playthrough or examine every object. It means a claimed ending needs evidence before being presented as fact. A reproducible walkthrough or developer comment could fill that gap.

Current story boundary: We know the setup (missing professor, bang, experiments, records), not a definitive cause or resolution.

10. Is the laboratory based on Tesla, Einstein or a specific science-fiction tradition?

Visually: The workshop includes experimental electrical machines, coils, drawings and old-school physics teaching furnishings S3S4. Those can reasonably evoke the historical laboratory imagery associated with Nikola Tesla, early twentieth-century physics, vintage classrooms and steampunk design.

Fact check: The sources consulted contain no confirmed UNIGINE statement that the unnamed professor is Tesla, Einstein or a historical person, that the setting represents an identifiable university, or that a specific film/game inspired the art. A picture of a celebrated scientist on the wall—if positively identified—would still be a portrait, not proof that the professor is that person.

The larger published context is clear: Superposition follows UNIGINE's outdoor Sanctuary, Tropics, Heaven and Valley demos, but chooses an enclosed, detail-rich lab mystery to show off its UNIGINE 2 engine, dynamic SSRTGI lighting and interactive physics S6S7. That change of stage helps give this benchmark a character beyond its score.

Further verification worth doing (not masquerading as completed)

  • Play and document Game Mode on the 1.1 build, photograph notebooks/chalkboard text and relevant objects; record whether a reproducible puzzle solution exists.
  • Check credits and development interviews for named level artists, reference photography and artistic influences; an artist post alone does not establish a whole-team design rationale.
  • Seek an official answer from UNIGINE if a canonical professor name, cause of the bang or location of the school matters.
